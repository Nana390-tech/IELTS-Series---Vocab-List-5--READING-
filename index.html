Reading — The Towers That Drink Fog

B1+ · General Studies, English Communications · the List 5 words in a passage

Lesson 1 — VocabularyLesson 2 — Reading
LevelB1+ (CEFR), IELTS Band 6 target Duration60 minutes
ClassEmirati students, 17–23 DateWeek of 18 October 2026
FocusFive IELTS question types · 20 marks FrameworkEngage · Explore · Explain · Extend · Evaluate
This lesson assumes Lesson 1 is done. Students should already know the 40 words. If some were absent, send them the quiz link before class — the passage uses the words without re-teaching them. If your session is 90 or 120 minutes, give Extend the extra time: run the paper once, review it as a class, then have them retake it from scratch. The second attempt is where the question types actually stick.

Lesson objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will…

  • label a diagram using only a written description of how something works;
  • explain the difference between False and Not Given, and give an example of each;
  • choose a heading that matches the main idea of a paragraph, not just a word inside it;
  • answer short questions in no more than three words taken from the passage;
  • use highlighting to mark the evidence for every answer you give.

Materials

  • APPReading Test 5 — The Towers That Drink Fog — 727-word passage, five teaching lessons, 20-mark paper, highlighter, certificate at 16/20
  • HUBThe Vocabulary Building Hub — to tick the reading test and light the News badge
  • ROOMProjector and student devices. No sound needed this lesson.
What the passage is about: a village with no water of its own, which now collects drinking water out of fog using tall panels of mesh. It is science news, so the List 5 words (approximately, limited, plenty, pure, scientific, engineering, forecast) appear naturally rather than being forced in.

The five phases

1

Engage — A village with no water

8 min

Do not show the passage yet. Set the problem first, so the solution is worth reading about.

TeacherStudents
  • Put one question on the board: “Your village has no water. There is no river, no well, and the lorry does not always come. What would you do?”
  • Take three or four answers. Expect: dig deeper, pipe it, desalinate, move away.
  • Then show only the title: The Towers That Drink Fog. Ask what it could possibly mean.
  • Tell them a desert beetle solved this before any engineer did. Leave it there.
  • In pairs, list three ways to get water to a village with none.
  • Read the title and predict: what are the towers, and how could they “drink”?
  • Write the prediction down — you will check it in ten minutes.
CBL evidence: the challenge is posed as a real problem before any text appears. The written prediction is what makes Explore purposeful rather than a skim.
2

Explore — First read, and the highlighter

12 min

They read for gist only. No questions yet — the questions come after they know what the passage says.

TeacherStudents
  • Students open the app and tap “Go straight to the test”. Tell them to ignore the questions for now.
  • Demonstrate the highlighter once on the projector: select text, tap Highlight, tap again to remove.
  • Set the three tasks. Six minutes, then take answers.
  • Ask whose Engage prediction was closest.
  • Read the whole passage once, quickly. Do not stop at unknown words.
  • Highlight the five List 5 words you can find. There are more than five.
  • In one sentence: how does a fog collector actually work?
  • Which paragraph would you look at to find out how much water the panels make?
CBL evidence: students locate their own prior vocabulary inside new text, which is the bridge from Lesson 1. The last question trains paragraph-level navigation before any question type is named.
3

Explain — Five question types, one real trap

15 min

The app has a five-lesson teaching phase. Let it do the explaining, and spend your own minutes on the one distinction that decides Band 6.

TypeThe trap the app names
Labelling a diagramGuessing from the picture instead of following the description
True / False / Not GivenChoosing False when the passage simply says nothing
Matching headingsPicking a heading because it repeats a word from the paragraph
Short-answer questionsCopying the first number you see — width and height are both there
Multiple choiceChoosing an option because every word in it appears in the passage

Stop the class after the T/F/NG lesson and teach this at the board (6 minutes)

TRUEThe passage says this.
FALSEThe passage says the opposite.
NOT GIVENThe passage says nothing either way.

Then put this on the board and take a show of hands before revealing the answer:

Passage: One panel produces approximately two hundred litres on a good foggy morning.
Statement: The panels cost less than bringing water by lorry.

Answer: NOT GIVEN. It may well be cheaper — but the passage never compares the two costs. Say the rule out loud and make them repeat it: if you cannot find a sentence, that is the answer.

Also worth 30 seconds: on the diagram, north is at the top, so west is on the left. That runs against the direction Arabic reads, and it is the single commonest error on plan and diagram tasks.
CBL evidence: teacher input is six minutes and targets the one distinction the data says costs the most marks. The app's lessons carry the other four types.
4

Extend — Sit the paper, then defend it

17 min
TeacherStudents
  • Set the 20-mark paper. Twelve minutes, working alone and in silence.
  • Insist on the highlighter: every answer needs its evidence marked.
  • Then stop them and set the pair task, before anyone submits.
  • Circulate during the pair task and listen for “it doesn't say” — that is the phrase you want.
  • Answer all 20 questions. Guess rather than leave a blank.
  • Highlight the sentence that proves each answer.
  • Pair task: swap devices. Find your partner's two Not Given answers and make them prove it — can they show you a sentence? If they can, it isn't Not Given.
  • Submit only after the pair check.
Why the pair task matters more than the score. A student who guesses Not Given correctly has learnt nothing. Asking them to fail to find a sentence — out loud, to a partner — is what turns the rule into something they can use in the exam.
CBL evidence: the paper is the application, and the pair task is the articulation. Together they give you both a mark and a reason.
5

Evaluate — Which type is costing you marks?

8 min
TeacherStudents
  • The app scores by question type and shows a bar for each. Project the class pattern.
  • Ask for a show of hands: whose lowest bar was T/F/NG? Expect most of the room.
  • Walk through two or three review items — the app shows the evidence sentence beside each answer.
  • Set the homework and close the two-lesson sequence.
  • Read the review screen, not just the score. Every answer has its evidence quoted.
  • 16 out of 20 earns the certificate.
  • Tick Reading test for List 5 on the hub — both ticks light the News badge.
  • Answer the exit ticket.

Exit ticket — three questions

  1. Explain the difference between False and Not Given to someone who missed today.
  2. On a diagram with north at the top, which side is west?
  3. Which question type was hardest for you, and what will you do differently next time?
CBL evidence: the per-type breakdown is objective evidence of where each student stands, and question 3 asks them to set their own next step — which is what Proficient looks like on the Evaluate row.

Differentiation

Needs supportGive them the five teaching lessons and the first nine questions only (diagram labelling and T/F/NG). Pair them with a stronger partner for the defence task. Let them use the hub's word page for any List 5 word they have forgotten.
ConfidentNo extra time, and ask them to justify every Not Given out loud. Then set the extension: write two new T/F/NG statements about the passage — one False, one Not Given — and test them on another pair.
AbsentSend the link. The app teaches the five types itself and saves a result file they can send you.

Anticipated problems

“It must be False”The commonest objection. Ask for the sentence. No sentence, no False.
Highlighter ignoredMake the pair task impossible without it — they cannot check a partner's evidence that was never marked.
Reading too slowlyThey read the whole passage in Explore already. Remind them the paper is about finding, not reading.
Short on timeCut the paper to questions 1–14 and set the rest as homework. Keep the pair task — it is the lesson.

Homework, and where the series goes next

  • Finish the paper and reach 16 out of 20.
  • Write three statements about the passage: one True, one False, one Not Given. Bring them in — we will test them on the class.
  • Both ticks on the hub give you the News badge. Four lists done, two to go.
